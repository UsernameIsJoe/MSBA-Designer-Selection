# MSBA Designer Selection Intelligence

Research platform for public Designer Selection Panel (DSP) results from the Massachusetts School Building Authority. It estimates which observable firm histories are associated with being ranked first among published finalists, checks those estimates against simple baselines, and keeps design quality, cost efficiency, and selection success as separate ideas.

The platform does not assume favoritism or misconduct. A low predicted probability is an unexpected model outcome, not a finding about the panel.

## What is estimated

The question that this archive can answer is: **given the firms the DSP published as finalists or as a ranked set, which firm was placed first?**

It does not estimate the probability of being shortlisted from the full applicant pool. Historical pages usually omit firms that were not advanced. Individual panelist votes are not published and are not invented. Architectural design quality is not directly measured. The linked designer construction cost chart is not in this snapshot, so cost performance is not scored. Schematic-design costs would also be known only after selection.

## Sources

Official pages and the local snapshots are listed in `src/msba_dsp/ingestion/sources.py` and hashed into `data/manifests/sources.csv` when the pipeline runs.

Included snapshots:

- DSP result pages for 2007–2009, 2010–2014, 2015–2018, and 2019–2024.
- The 2025–2026 DSP meeting result chart (applicants, shortlists, ranks, and preselection cost ranges).
- The January 2026 procedures PDF and the public process page, used to describe the stated criteria. They are not result tables.

Not included, and therefore not used in a model:

- Designer construction cost chart (PDF and Tableau).
- Capital-planning project and cost portals.
- Firm websites, headquarters, or marketing claims. Present-day company profiles are stored in `data/reference/firm_company.csv` and are not covariates in the winner model.

## Run

```powershell
python -m pip install -e ".[dev]"
python run_pipeline.py
python -m pytest
```

`MSBA_BOOTSTRAP` controls the number of competition-resampled draws (default 40). `MSBA_SEED` defaults to `20261020`.

The pipeline rereads the local HTML and Excel snapshots, writes processed tables and `data/processed/msba_dsp.sqlite`, writes `reports/`, and refreshes `dashboard/platform.js`.

Open the dashboard with a local server so the browser can load the scripts:

```powershell
python -m http.server 8765 --directory dashboard
```

## How the sample is built

A competition enters the winner model only when the publication lists at least two firms and exactly one top-ranked firm. Repair pools, informational interviews, and unranked shortlists are stored and are not treated as wins.

When a page says a list is a ranking but prints bullets instead of numerals, rank follows the published order and the row is flagged `published_list_order`.

Firm names lose only case, accents, punctuation, and legal suffixes automatically. Further matches are an explicit reviewed list in `src/msba_dsp/processing/names.py`. Joint ventures and “in association with” lines keep their own identifiers. The alias audit is `data/processed/firm_alias_audit.csv`.

Experience variables use earlier calendar dates only. Two selections on the same day do not update each other. A firm with no earlier row is an archive newcomer, not necessarily a new practice. The archive starts in 2007.

## Models

| Model | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Equal probability, prior wins, grade wins, shrunk win rate | Baselines |
| Conditional logit | Primary interpretable model. Probabilities sum to one inside each choice set. |
| Penalized firm effects | Partial pooling. A residual after experience covariates, not a quality score. |
| Choice-set-normalized gradient boosting | Nonlinear check. Importance is not causal. |
| Plackett-Luce rating | Historical selection strength when firms meet. Walk-forward ratings use only earlier years. |

Out-of-sample scores for 2019 onward are fit on competitions through 2018. The project does not use a random split across time. Top-choice accuracy requires a unique favorite, so an equal-probability baseline scores zero on that metric. Log loss is the measure that compares it with the other models.

Project-level facts such as year or grade do not, by themselves, choose a winner. They enter only through interactions with a firm attribute.

- `reports/02_firm_characteristics.md` — Phase 2 comparison of present-day staff size, headquarters, and firm age inside finalist lists.
- `reports/03_incumbency_specialization.md` — Phase 3 comparison of earlier rank-1 results and same-type experience.
- `reports/04_selection_stages.md` — Phase 4 conversion from the 2025–2026 applicant lists to interview lists and rank 1.
- `reports/05_panel_history.md` — Phase 5 audit of the inferred panel snapshots and the published rankings in the years each snapshot covers.
- `reports/06_project_performance.md` — Phase 6 feasibility of using public cost files as a performance history.

## Findings so far

Stage 0 through Phase 6 are complete. The synthesis is `reports/MSBA_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`. Rank 1 remains a published panel order, not a signed contract, and an unexplained pattern is not a finding about favoritism or design quality.

The winner-model sample reproduces as 273 multi-firm rankings, 794 finalist rows, and 82 firms, from 2007-08-07 through 2026-09-22. Forty-two firms rank first at least once. The rank-1 Herfindahl index is 0.045, the effective number of winners is about 22, and the five most frequent rank-1 firms account for 35% of rankings (bootstrap interval 32% to 42%). Finalist appearances are at least as spread out. After 2011, about nine in ten rank-1 results go to a firm that had already ranked first in this archive. That persistence is descriptive. The existing winner model still finds the out-of-sample case for prior wins inconclusive, because the experience logit does not beat an equal-probability forecast on 2019–2026 competitions.

The robust change in market structure is the length of the list. Three-firm rankings are 85% of the sample. Two-firm rankings rise from 7 of 138 competitions in 2009–2016 to 23 of 101 in 2018–2025. A decline in five-year winner concentration is the direction of the point estimates and is not resolved by the bootstrap.

Company staff size and office location are 2026 research fields. They are not in the winner model. Among finalists with a verified staff band, the firm in the highest current band is rank 1 about as often as chance (gap −2.8 points, interval −11 to +6). Age at selection, from a recorded founding year, is likewise flat. Boston-city headquarters rank first less often than their share of located finalists, and other Massachusetts headquarters rank first more often, but that gap includes zero once Cambridge and Somerville are grouped with Boston.

From 2012, in lists that contain both a firm with an earlier rank-1 result in this archive and a firm without one, the firms that already have one take the top place 17 points more often than their share of the field (interval 8 to 26). The rate on a later appearance is about 20% with no earlier rank-1 result and about 38% with three or more. That is an association across appearances, not a demonstration that the first result caused the later ones. On 93 known-type competitions from 2019 on, a shrunk rank-1 rate beats an equal chance by a small margin. Adding same-type wins, wins from the previous five years, or present-day staff and headquarters does not improve the forecast by a margin the test set resolves. School type here corrects the stored labels: Middleborough High School is a high school, and a vocational technical high school is vocational. Names that do not state a grade stay unclassified.

The 2025–2026 chart is the first source that lists applicants who were not interviewed. On 27 projects the median applicant pool is 5 and the median interview list is 3. Every completed ranking is a list of two or three firms. Firms with three or more earlier rank-1 results are interviewed on 57 of 92 applications (62%, interval 54% to 71%), and firms with none on 13 of 38 (34%, 22% to 49%). That gap shows up in the pre-interview number: a number of 1 or 2 is almost always interviewed, and a firm with no number rarely is. Historical HTML lists are interview lists, not applicant pools, so this cut is not estimated for 2007–2024. Same-type experience, 2026 staff size, and a Boston address do not mark a separate stage in this chart.

The inferred DSP roster is five carried snapshots, not a ballot and not an attendance record. The October 2026 roster page lists 18 names. The process page describes 13 appointed members plus three unnamed local members, and the roster page does not say which 13 of the 18 vote. Three named people appear on both the 2018 snapshot and the 2026 page. During the years covered by the 2013 snapshot, 4 of 79 rankings are two-firm lists. During the years covered by the 2018 snapshot, 20 of 88 are two-firm lists (23%, interval 15% to 32%). That difference is 17.7 points (8.2 to 28.2). The 2018 snapshot is the uncertain one: it may include staff, and it does not name a chair. Firms that already have a rank-1 result in this archive take the top place at a similar rate in every window.

The public designer cost chart is a one-page graphic of average schematic direct-building cost per square foot. The dollar value for each firm is a drawn line, not a number in the text. The capital-planning sheets have project columns, and the current schematic files are labeled as of the June 2026 Board meeting, with a warning that bids and amendments can change the figures. A schematic budget exists only after the designer was selected for that project. Four rankings in the model sample are dated after that June 2026 label. No performance model was fit. The preselection cost range already on the 2025–2026 meeting chart is a project-size band, not a record of how an earlier school turned out.

src/msba_dsp/        ingestion, resolution, models, market structure, firm characteristics, incumbency, selection stages, and panel snapshots

## Updating

Add a new MSBA result snapshot under `data/raw`, point `src/msba_dsp/ingestion/sources.py` at it, and rerun `python run_pipeline.py`. Keep the previous file if you replace a page, and let the manifest record the new hash. Do not fill missing votes, costs, or firm attributes by hand inside the model tables.
