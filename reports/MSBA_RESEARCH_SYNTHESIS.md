# MSBA designer selection, 2007–2026

What the public result lists can say about firm characteristics, earlier results, competitive structure, and the panel, and what they leave open.

Rank 1 is the published order of qualifications. The district awards the contract. An unexplained pattern is not favoritism, misconduct, or architectural quality. Counts below use the winner-model sample unless a sentence names another denominator: 273 multi-firm rankings, 794 finalist rows, 82 firms, 2007-08-07 through 2026-09-22. The stored winner model was not refit.

## A. Market structure

**The rank-1 set is broad.** The Herfindahl index is 0.045, about 22 firms would match that concentration, and the five most frequent rank-1 firms account for 95 of 273 rankings (35%, bootstrap interval 32% to 42%). Forty-two firms rank first at least once. Evidence: descriptive, with a competition bootstrap on the top-five share. A decline in five-year concentration is the direction of the point estimates and is not resolved by that bootstrap.

**Published lists got shorter.** Three-firm rankings are 85% of the sample. Two-firm rankings rise from 7 of 138 in 2009–2016 to 23 of 101 in 2018–2025, a difference of 17.7 points (9.1 to 26.4). The same difference appears when the cut is the roster snapshots: 4 of 79 in the years covered by the 2013 snapshot, and 20 of 88 in the years covered by the 2018 snapshot (17.7 points, 8.2 to 28.2). Evidence: the change in list length is resolved. The cause is not. On the 2025–2026 chart, which is the first source that lists firms that were not interviewed, the median applicant pool is 5 and the median interview list is 3, and every completed ranking is two or three firms. That mechanism is not observed for 2007–2024. Historical pages usually publish the interviewed firms and omit the rest.

**After 2011, rank-1 results stay inside the firms that have already ranked first.** About nine in ten do. Evidence: descriptive. The archive starts in 2007, so a firm can have ranked first before the files begin.

## B. Competitive advantage

**An earlier rank-1 result in this archive is associated with a higher later rate, inside the list.** From 2012, on 68 lists that contain both a firm with an earlier rank-1 result and a firm without one, the firms that already have one take the top place 17 points more often than their share of the field (8 to 26). On later appearances the rate is about 20% with no earlier rank-1 result and about 38% with three or more. Evidence: the within-list gap's interval excludes zero. It does not show that the first result caused the later ones. A firm's win rate before its first win is zero by construction, so that rate is not a before-and-after control.

**Out of sample, the gain over an equal chance is small.** On 93 known-type competitions from 2019 on, a shrunk rank-1 rate beats equal probability by about 0.03 log-loss points, and that gap's interval excludes zero. A prior-wins logit does not: its gap against equal probability includes zero, which matches the stored model's inconclusive label for prior wins. The stored experience logit is worse than equal probability on the 2019–2026 test (1.077 against 1.014). Evidence: one small forecast gain for the shrunk rate; the linear prior-wins model remains inconclusive.

**Same-type experience, recent wins, 2026 staff size, and a 2026 address do not add a resolved forecast.** Adding same-type wins to prior wins changes the test loss by 0.006 (−0.010 to 0.022). Wins in the previous five years, and present-day staff and headquarters coded with missing as a reference level, likewise fail to beat the simpler forecast by a margin the test set resolves. Among finalists with a verified staff band, the firm in the highest current band is rank 1 about as often as chance (gap −2.8 points, −11 to 6). The Boston-city deficit and the surplus for other Massachusetts headquarters include zero once Cambridge and Somerville are grouped with Boston. Evidence: inconclusive, and the attributes are 2026 research fields. School labels in this comparison correct the stored categories: Middleborough High School is a high school, and a vocational technical high school is vocational. Names that do not state a grade stay unclassified. The stored model's "supported" label for same-grade wins depends on a contrast that `reports/research_report.md` does not print. That label was not changed.

**On the 2025–2026 chart, the interview cut follows the pre-interview number.** Firms with three or more earlier rank-1 results are interviewed on 57 of 92 applications (62%, 54% to 71%). Firms with none are interviewed on 13 of 38 (34%, 22% to 49%). Firms numbered 1 are interviewed 29 of 29 times, firms numbered 2 are interviewed 27 of 29 times, and firms with no number are interviewed 6 of 63 times. Firms with three or more earlier results receive a 1 or a 2 on 47 of 92 applications; firms with none do so on 7 of 38. Once a firm has a 1 or a 2, the interview list does not drop the inexperienced firms further. After the interview, 22 of 23 completed rank-1 firms already have an earlier result on the stored firm id. The exception is the chart spelling "Flansburg Architects," kept separate from Flansburgh Associates. Evidence: descriptive rates with project bootstraps, on this chart only. The applicant pool before 2025 is not in the files.

**Penalized firm effects improve the stored forecast and are still selection residuals.** Out-of-sample log loss is 0.941 with firm effects, 1.077 for the experience logit, and 0.979 for the shrunk rate. Nine firms have a residual interval excluding zero and at least eight appearances. Evidence: the forecast gain is the stored result. The residual is not a quality score.

## C. Institutional continuity

**The membership file is five snapshots, carried across the other years in each block.** Confidence in the file is inferred for 2007–2009, carried-forward for 2010–2012 and 2013–2017, uncertain for 2018–2024, and mixed for 2025–2026. Carried years are not attendance. The 2013 building-trades vacancy is a seat note, not a person, and carrying it forward is not a confirmed five-year vacancy. Evidence: an audit of the supplied file against the October 20, 2026 roster page. The public page on October 21, 2026 listed the same 18 names.

**The October 2026 page lists more names than the 13 appointed seats.** Twelve names are not labeled staff, five are labeled MSBA staff, and John F. Jumpe Jr. is a staff alternate to one of three staff seats. The process page describes 13 appointed members plus three local members. The roster page does not say which 13 vote. The three local members are unnamed on every project. Evidence: the two public pages disagree in count, and neither page is a ballot.

**Named overlap is stable until the last snapshot, and the outcome that moves is list length.** Nine, eight, and eight named people appear on both sides of the first three snapshot boundaries. Three names appear on both the 2018 snapshot and the 2026 page: Karl Brown, Russell Bartash, and John Jumpe Jr. The 2026 page labels Brown as staff and Jumpe as a staff alternate. The 2018 snapshot may include staff and does not name a chair. The two-firm increase sits on that boundary. The excess for firms that already have a rank-1 result does not: the change from the 2013–2017 window to the 2018–2024 window is −3.9 points (−25.3 to 17.8). Appearances that already have a rank-1 result take the top place on 34% to 42% of those appearances in every window. Evidence: the membership lists are snapshots. The list-length change is the Phase 1 fact. It is not identified as an effect of who sat.

No member's preference is estimated. Former employers are not in the collected files. A 2026 designation is not read backward onto an earlier snapshot.

## D. Project outcomes

**A performance model was not fit.** The designer cost chart is a graphic of average schematic direct-building cost per square foot. The number for each firm is a drawn line, not text. Three opened vintages cover board approvals in July 2016–June 2021, calendar 2020–2024, and calendar 2021–2025. The line excludes sitework, demolition, abatement, markups, and escalation, and one project is left off an older chart because it exceeded the axis.

The capital-planning sheets do have project columns: about 171 in the June 2026 schematic files for four school types, and about 136 board-approval dates in the current bid files. The publisher says those figures may have changed because of bids or amendments, and a schematic budget exists only after the designer was selected for that project. Four model rankings are dated after the June 2026 vintage printed on the schematic files. Evidence: the files were opened and counted. They do not support a lagged comparison in which the covariate is something the panel could have known. Detail is in `reports/06_project_performance.md`.

The preselection cost range on the 2025–2026 chart remains a project-size band, not a performance history. The stored cost-performance hypothesis stays inconclusive.

- The contract the district actually signed, as distinct from rank 1.

Near-duplicate firm names that were left unmerged, including Flansburg Architects beside Flansburgh Associates, can move a few appearances. ## F. A price on the published order

**A price proportional to earlier rank-1 counts is too sharp, and a softer exponent chosen on earlier years beats an equal chance later.** From 2012, 185 of 204 lists have one firm strictly ahead on earlier rank-1 counts. That firm is placed first on 94 of them, 15.1 points above its seat share (8.0 to 21.9). On the 588 finalist rows, a proportional price under 20% is placed first 22.9% of the time (gap +12.8 points, 6.8 to 18.8), and a price from 50% to 65% is placed first 41.3% of the time (gap −16.0 points, −26.8 to −4.7). Exponent 0.35 minimizes log loss on the 105 rankings from 2012 through 2018. On the 99 rankings from 2019 on it beats an equal chance by 0.064 log-loss points (0.016 to 0.111). Exponent 1 does not. The softer price's gain over the stored shrunk rate includes zero. When the favorite loses a three-firm list, second place and last place are not separated (44 second and 36 last out of 80; the interval on the second-place share includes one half). Detail is in `reports/09_price_calibration.md`. The stored winner model was not refit.

## G. How hard to weight the history

**Same-grade wins earn a weight. The four history items earn a weight only at half strength. Appearances on their own make the forecast worse.** The stored logit uses its weights at full strength and loses to an equal chance on the rankings from 2019 on (1.077 against 1.014). Weights fit through 2015, with the strength chosen on 2016–2018, change that. Same-grade rank-1 results at three-quarter strength beat an equal chance on the 99 later rankings by 0.066 log-loss points (0.016 to 0.120). The four history items at half strength beat it by 0.058 (0.017 to 0.102). Full strength of those four does not: the interval includes zero. A weight on earlier finalist appearances, used alone, is worse than an equal chance by 0.042 (0.017 to 0.066). The stored extras for list length, year, and high school, even at half strength, are not resolved. Detail is in `reports/10_weight_dial.md`. The stored winner model was not refit.
